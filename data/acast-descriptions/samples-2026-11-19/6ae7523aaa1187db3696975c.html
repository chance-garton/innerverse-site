Does a Renaissance painting contain evidence that nearly 200 years of history were invented? Chance Garton and Dylan Saccoccio investigate Anatoly Fomenko’s New Chronology through the zodiac ceiling of the Sala di Galatea at Rome’s Villa Farnesina.

The conventional interpretation connects the ceiling’s horoscope to Agostino Chigi’s birth in 1466. Fomenko proposes 1656. That gap leads to bigger claims: phantom duplicates of historical figures, a powerful pope’s family rewriting Italian history, and works attributed to Raphael being reassigned to Carlo Maratta.

We examine the artwork panel by panel, compare interpretations of its mythological and planetary symbolism, and check the proposed dates against the sky.
