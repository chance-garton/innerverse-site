Sound and electrical response

Electric currents produce magnetism, and if that current changes, the resulting magnetism is also affected. The reverse is true too. If you influence a magnetic field, it changes the voltage of electric current. Voltage is the push, current is the flow.

The standard model of physics says that sound waves are mechanical in nature, meaning that sound is caused by molecules bouncing off each other and causing waves of high and low pressure. But the same physics also says that on the atomic level, nothing is actually bouncing off anything, and that the whole process is electromagnetic, no atoms ever touch other atoms.

So, depending on who you ask and how you ask it, you may be told sound is or is not electromagnetic. If sound has a magnetic effect, it could explain why people feel their energy flow improve from tuning. Magnetism affects electric currents in the body and voltage, the push of that current, increases.

Personally, I’m highly skeptical of the atomic theory. Electromagnetism however is something we can all observe without needing degrees and expensive equipment. And anyone who has experienced tuning can tell you, something is definitely happening to their energy.

I’d like to avoid trying to explain things through the theory of atoms and tiny particles. What we can say is that hearing sound depends on sound waves being converted to electrical signals. This extends beyond just the ears though.

The piezoelectric effect is the ability of certain materials to generate an electric charge when subjected to various forms of mechanical stress, like being squeezed or vibrated. This effect has been measured in biological matter. Large structures in our body, like our skeleton, and smaller components in our cells are all subject to this effect, and this piezoelectric charge can be generated from sound waves.

Eileen wrote about this in her paper, The Physics of Pushback.

“Many of these components are piezoelectric or otherwise electromechanically active. When they are stretched, compressed, or twisted, they generate electrical signals. Those signals in turn alter local and global field patterns. The fields can feed back into mechanical behavior. The result is a tightly coupled field-structure system.”

Eileen Day McKusick, The Physics of Pushback

Why is this important? Because it establishes the biofeedback loop that is a big part of how tuning works.

Bodies of Light

One of the most fascinating aspects of the electromagnetic nature of our biology is the phenomenon called biophoton emission. In other words, the body produces light. Measuring it requires very sensitive cameras, because it is too faint to be detected by our eyes.

This light emission can be measured at all times, but certain conditions have been shown to increase the amount of light that bodies release. Scientists who have studied the phenomenon tend to focus on things like cellular metabolism, oxidative stress, and reactive chemistry. It’s as if “burning calories” is literal, and metabolic activity puts off more light as it ramps up, akin to the difference in light from gently burning coals versus throwing dry brush on a fire.

On the non-physical side of biophoton emission, research has demonstrated psychological stress and acute negative emotional states, like anxiety and anger, actually alter the intensity and patterning of biological light emission, usually causing them to become more chaotic.

And the inverse is true too, calming down, meditating, and relaxation practices have been shown to tone down the intensity of biophotons and make their emission patterns more orderly.

The seven energy centers

You’ve probably heard of chakras before. Chakras are seven conceptual energy centers, generally located in specific points from the base of the spine up to the top of the head. When symptoms persist chronically, it therefore means that whatever behavior is causing the imbalance has not been changed. It can additionally indicate that the body lacks the energy to complete the process it needs to do for full resolution, and the symptoms reflect a holding pattern. We should understand that the body does not undertake any healing process that it doesn’t have the energetic budget to complete.

When we think of our energy in this economic framework, the expense of repressing emotion becomes much more significant. As Eileen described, emotion itself is energy in a particular, polarized form. When we repress that emotion because we don’t want to express it or feel it, the subconscious mental barriers we erect to keep it away are also energy. It’s like delegating a certain amount of your focus to the repression. So stuck emotions are doubly expensive, as the energy in the form of emotion, and the energy in the form of the barrier, both become unavailable to us.

Reclaiming energy

“It’s important to understand that the energy we are putting back in the body is not “bad” or “negative.” It is energy that had been trapped in an incoherent pattern in the field and is inherently neutral.”

Eileen Day McKusick, Tuning the Human Biofield, pp. 230-231

“The goal is not to get rid of all these emotions so that we are no longer emotional. You’re still going to be emotional.”

Eileen Day McKusick, Electric Body, Electric Health, p. 114

If our life force energy is our mind, our memory, and the electricity that runs our biology, every metaphorical drop that we lose track of becomes significant. And because our available budget of energy is on a daily cycle, that loss is cumulative over time. You can think of it like your paycheck. What would be the long term consequence of getting docked ten percent of your weekly salary? Or simply ask yourself what you could do if you had ten percent more energy on average, every day, for a year. Obviously, this adds up!

My favorite metaphor is the one where you imagine the course of your life as being like a ship at sea. If your ship changes course, even by just one degree to the east or west, your ship will be in a very different place after enough time passes than it would have been, had you kept up the old heading.

Energy and awareness

Apart from mundane energy budgets, there’s also the spiritual factor to consider. Your life force energy is your mind. It’s the spirit that animates your vessel. It’s your consciousness. Think about it. When you are tired, you lose some amount of awareness based on how tired you are. You see less far, you notice less readily, things are more likely to go in one ear, out the other. In other words, less energy, less consciousness.

In the other direction, the more rested and full of energy you are, the more conscious you are. And once you pass a certain threshold of energy, the consciousness benefits begin to go beyond just basic sensory awareness. The more energy you can hold, the more of yourself you are. You remember more about why you came into this life. You feel a sense of your purpose for being, what you really want out of life.

And with this vitality comes intelligence too. Intelligence is not a value that each person is arbitrarily assigned when they are born, and permanently stuck with forever. Intelligence is a function of health. Sleepwalking because of low energy makes it very, very difficult to know what you truly want. This aimless, low consciousness state is, regrettably, the default mode for most western people in our time, and it’s completely reversible.
